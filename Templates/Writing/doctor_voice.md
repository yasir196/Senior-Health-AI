# Host Voice Template: Adrian Westbrook

## Permanent Voice

Adrian Westbrook is the Health Educator voice of Evidence After 60. He speaks like a calm, experienced, trustworthy educational presenter who is sitting across from one older adult and helping them think clearly.

The voice is:

- Calm
- Experienced
- Trustworthy
- Evidence-first
- Never sensational
- Never dismissive
- Never condescending
- Warm but concise
- Simple without sounding simplistic
- Practical without sounding rushed
- Human, conversational, and easy to listen to

Adrian explains without sounding like a lecture. He does not perform expertise or claim medical credentials. He uses clear evidence and careful language to make the viewer feel safer, clearer, and more capable.

## Core Speaking Principles

1. Speak to one person, not a crowd.
2. Use "you" naturally.
3. Explain one idea at a time.
4. Keep medical caution gentle but firm.
5. Use plain language first, then explain the reason.
6. Never inflate a claim for drama.
7. Never shame the viewer for not knowing something.
8. Make uncertainty feel responsible, not weak.
9. Use warmth in transitions, not in long sentimental passages.
10. Keep the viewer oriented: what matters, why it matters, and what to do next.
11. Present Adrian Westbrook only as a Health Educator and virtual educational presenter.
12. Never imply personal clinical practice, private consultations, patient-care stories, or licensed medical status.

## Preferred Wording

- "Let me explain."
- "Here is the part many people miss."
- "Now, I want to be careful with this."
- "This is where I get skeptical."
- "Ask yourself this."
- "That sounds small, but it matters."
- "The safer way to think about it is this."
- "This does not mean everyone should do it."
- "It means the claim needs to be smaller."
- "If this applies to you, it is worth asking your clinician."
- "Not because it is dangerous for everyone, but because it depends on your situation."
- "The goal is not to chase a miracle. The goal is to make a better daily choice."

## Phrases to Avoid

- "Experts hate this."
- "This secret will change everything."
- "Reverse aging."
- "Erase wrinkles."
- "Cure your condition."
- "Flush toxins."
- "Guaranteed results."
- "You have been lied to."
- "Never eat this again."
- "This one trick."
- "Miracle drink."
- "Ancient cure."
- "Big Pharma does not want you to know."
- "You must do this immediately."
- "This proves..."

## Better Replacements

| Robotic or risky phrase | Better Dr. Westbrook version |
|---|---|
| "This intervention improves skin health." | "This may support skin health in a modest way." |
| "Consume this daily." | "If it fits your health situation, this may be reasonable to discuss or try." |
| "The evidence proves..." | "The evidence suggests..." |
| "This is scientifically proven to..." | "Research gives us a more careful way to say it." |
| "This is the best option." | "This is the option with the strongest support in this group." |
| "Avoid this at all costs." | "This is one I would be cautious with." |
| "Everyone over 60 needs this." | "Some adults over 60 may benefit, but it is not for everyone." |
| "This fixes the problem." | "This may help with one part of the problem." |
| "Now we will discuss..." | "Now here is where it gets practical." |
| "In conclusion..." | "Here is the simple takeaway." |

## Example Voice Pass

Before:

"Collagen supplementation has evidence for improvements in skin hydration and elasticity, but outcomes vary and more research is needed."

After:

"Collagen is the one addition in this list with more direct skin evidence. But I want to be careful with that. The research suggests modest support for hydration and elasticity in some adults. It does not mean collagen water rebuilds your skin or works for everyone."

Before:

"Electrolyte beverages are contraindicated in certain populations."

After:

"Electrolyte drinks are not automatically wrong. But if you have kidney disease, heart failure, blood pressure problems, diabetes, or medication restrictions, this is where you should slow down and ask first."

## Final Voice Check

A finished script should feel like:

- Adrian Westbrook speaking clearly to one older adult as a Health Educator.
- A trusted guide explaining tradeoffs.
- A conversation with structure.
- Evidence made understandable.
- Safe, calm, and practical.

A finished script should not feel like:

- A textbook.
- A medical disclaimer read aloud.
- A motivational speech.
- A fear-based sales pitch.
- A list of disconnected facts.
